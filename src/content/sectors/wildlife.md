# Wildlife & National Parks

Tanzania is home to some of Africa's most iconic national parks and conservation areas, hosting the largest mammal migration on Earth and the highest densities of big cats on the continent. From the endless plains of the Serengeti to the volcanic caldera of Ngorongoro, the country's protected area network covers roughly 38% of national land — a global leader in conservation allocation.

## Key Facts

- **Serengeti National Park** — 14,763 km², UNESCO World Heritage Site (1981), home to the Great Wildebeest Migration (≈1.5 million wildebeest + 200,000 zebra + 300,000 Thomson's gazelle annually).
- **Ngorongoro Conservation Area** — 8,292 km², contains the world's largest unbroken volcanic caldera (260 km² floor, 600 m deep). Multiple land-use zone: wildlife + semi-nomadic Maasai pastoralists. Managed by NCAA, not TANAPA.
- **Nyerere (Selous) Game Reserve** — 50,000 km², Africa's largest protected area. UNESCO World Heritage Site (1982) and a critical habitat for African wild dogs (~1,300 of the continent's ~6,600).
- **Tarangire National Park** — 2,850 km², famous for elephant herds (≈3,000 individuals) and ancient baobab trees.
- **Ruaha National Park** — 20,226 km², Tanzania's second-largest park and the country's largest elephant population (~10,000+).
- **Lake Manyara National Park** — 330 km², ground-water forest and tree-climbing lions.
- TANAPA manages 22 national parks; NCAA manages Ngorongoro + Lake Natron area.

## Statistics

- Tanzania hosts roughly **~25% of Africa's large mammal biomass**.
- **~38%** of Tanzania's land area is under formal protection (parks, reserves, game-controlled areas).
- Wildebeest migration: ~800 km circuit, calving season Jan–Mar in Ndutu, river crossings Jul–Sep in northern Serengeti.
- Annual park fee for foreign adult non-resident (Serengeti, 2024/25): **USD 70/day** (park entry) + concession/camping fees.
- Ngorongoro crater service fee (non-resident vehicle): **USD 295/vehicle/day** plus **USD 70/person/day**.
