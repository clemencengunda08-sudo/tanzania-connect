# Culture & Etiquette

Tanzania is one of the most ethnically diverse countries in Africa, with >125 ethnic groups and a unifying national language (Kiswahili). About 65% of the population is Christian, 30% Muslim (almost all in Zanzibar and along the coast, where Islam is dominant), 5% traditional/other. Social life is built on politeness, group harmony, and respect for elders; casual Western directness can feel rude. The "pole pole" (slowly, slowly) pace is part of life.

## Key Facts

- **National language**: Kiswahili (Swahili) — spoken by ~95% of Tanzanians as L1 or L2.
- **Official languages**: Swahili + English (used in higher courts, universities, business).
- **Greeting**: A handshake is universal. Direct "hi, how are you" is OK, but a fuller greeting is "Habari? (What news?) — Nzuri (Good). Habari za kazi? (How's work?) — Nzuri, asante." Take time with it.
- **Bismillah / "Mungu"**: Muslim greetings "Salaam Aleikum / Aleikum Salaam" common in Zanzibar and Dar. Christians may use "Shikamoo" (I hold your feet) to elders as a sign of respect.
- **Dress code**: 
  - In cities: smart-casual. Light fabrics for the heat, but cover shoulders in mosques, churches, and government buildings.
  - On Zanzibar & coast: cover knees/shoulders away from the beach; women especially should avoid short skirts/shorts.
  - Maasai areas: ask before photographing; do not wear camouflage (illegal).
  - For business: a collared shirt or smart top, closed-toe shoes; women in dresses or trousers are both fine.
- **Religion**: Sunday is the Christian Sabbath; Friday the Islamic Jumu'ah. Ramadan is widely observed and respected (avoid eating/drinking in public before dusk).
- **Family / hierarchy**: respect for elders is paramount; children often defer. Public disagreement is rare — use intermediaries for conflict.
- **Tipping**: not obligatory but appreciated; 5–10% in restaurants; USD 5–10/day for safari guides and house staff.
- **Time and pacing**: time is more flexible than in the West — meetings often start 15–30 minutes late. Do not push or show annoyance.
- **Music & dance**: ngoma (drums), taarab (Zanzibar coastal music with Arab/Indian influence), bongo flava (modern pop with hip-hop, R&B), muziki wa dansi. Famous names: Diamond Platnumz, Ali Kiba, Harmonize.
- **Food staples**: ugali (maize porridge), wali na maharage (rice and beans), nyama choma (grilled meat), chipsi mayai, mishkaki (skewers), biryani (coast), Zanzibar pizza (street food), ndizi (plantain).
- **Cuisine**: vegetarian-friendly — chipsi mayai, ndizi, maharage, ndafu (coconut bean stew), viazi karai (potato fritters).
- **Festivals**: 
  - **Sauti za Busara** (Feb, Zanzibar) — pan-African music festival.
  - **Zanzibar International Film Festival (ZIFF)** (Jul) — the Dhow Countries Film Festival.
  - **Mwaka Kogwa** (Jul, Zanzibar) — Persian/Zanzibari New Year fire festival.
  - **Maasai cultural festivals** in Arusha/Monduli (Jun/Jul).
  - **Eid al-Fitr / Eid al-Hajj** (lunar dates) — public holidays.
  - **Nane Nane** (Aug 8) — Farmers' Day.
  - **Saba Saba** (Jul 7) — Industrial/Workers' Day (Dar trade fair).
  - **Uhuru/Independence Day** (Dec 9) — major public celebration.

## Statistics

- Population: ~67 million (2024 est.).
- 125+ ethnic groups: Sukuma (largest, ~16%), Chagga, Haya, Nyamwezi, Maasai, Makonde, Yao, Hehe, Gogo, Bena, etc.
- Christians: ~63%, Muslims ~34%, traditional/other ~3% (Zanzibar >95% Muslim).
- Average household size: ~5.
- Urban / rural split: ~35 / 65.
