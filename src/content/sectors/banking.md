# Banking & Mobile Money

Tanzania's financial sector is anchored by Bank of Tanzania (BoT, the central bank), a small group of large commercial banks, and a fast-growing mobile-money ecosystem that has leapfrogged traditional banking for retail users. As of 2025, six banks — CRDB, NMB, NBC, Equity, Stanbic and Exim — hold the bulk of commercial bank assets, and three mobile network operators (Vodacom/M-Pesa, Tigo/Airtel merged under Airtel Money, and Halotel) dominate digital payments.

## Key Facts

- **Currency**: Tanzanian Shilling (TZS). Approximate rate in 2025–26: USD 1 ≈ TZS 2,500–2,600.
- **Central bank**: Bank of Tanzania (BoT) — www.bot.go.tz. Issues monetary policy, supervises banks, and operates the Tanzania Instant Payment System (TIPS).
- **Top commercial banks**: NMB Bank, CRDB Bank, NBC (National Bank of Commerce, partly state-owned), Standard Chartered, Exim Bank, Equity Bank Tanzania, Stanbic Bank.
- **Mobile money**: M-Pesa (Vodacom, ~12+ million active wallets), Tigo Pesa / Airtel Money (now merged under Airtel Money after the 2024 merger), Halopesa (Halotel). All three are BoT-licensed mobile-money operators.
- **TIPS (Tanzania Instant Payment System)**: launched 2023; allows near-instant, low-cost wallet-to-wallet and bank-to-wallet transfers 24/7.
- **DIB (Deposit Insurance Board)**: covers commercial-bank depositors up to TZS 1,500,000 (≈USD 580). Mobile-money wallets are NOT covered by DIB.

## Statistics

- Banking penetration (adults with formal account): ~50% (2022 FinScope), mobile-money penetration: ~65% of adults.
- CRDB Bank: largest commercial bank by assets (~TZS 9 trillion+ in 2024).
- NMB Bank: largest retail network, ~700+ branches and agents across the country.
- TIPS processed multi-billion TZS daily volume in 2024; mobile-money transactions exceeded TZS 100 trillion/year.
- Foreign-exchange controls: residents/visitors must declare ≥USD 10,000 on entry/exit; BoT-licensed FX bureaus available at airports and city centers.
